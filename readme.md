Conclusion We propose SubspaceNet, which enables subspace-based DOA estimation in challenging settings where a reliable partition of the array input into signal and noise subspaces is not feasible. SubspaceNet is designed by enhancing the subspace method with deep learning tools, specifically trained to recognize Root-MUSIC as an appropriate differentiable method and convert it into a trainable architecture, producing a general useful alternative covariance for subspace-based DOA estimation. SubspaceNet utilizes data to learn how to partition the observation results into signal and noise subspaces. Our results show that it can successfully cope with coherent sources, broadband signals, few snapshots, low SNR, and array misalignment while preserving the interpretability of model-based subspace methods and promoting operation of other covariance-based algorithms (e.g., MVDR).
